# EXP-13 — the sample rate is fine; the period estimator is not

- **Date:** 2026-08-18
- **Unit:** bench unit #1
- **Build:** `make guest-coldtrace`, build stamp `Aug 18 2026 17:21:17` — the
  first image carrying `scope_timebase.c`
- **Status:** CONFIRMED — and it **reverts the Freq badge** shipped an hour
  earlier in the same session

## 1. Problem

`scope_timebase.c` landed with measured sample rates, and the measurement
badges were wired to print `Freq = fs / period_samples` and period in seconds.
Both factors were assumed sound: the rate because it is fitted and
fold-checked, the period because it has been on the screen for weeks.

Before declaring that done, does the badge actually agree with a known drive?

## 2. Hypothesis

Freq should read the drive frequency.

- **If both factors are sound:** the badge tracks the generator within a few
  percent across codes and frequencies.
- **If not:** the discrepancy identifies which factor is at fault — the rate is
  independently known, so any error belongs to the period.

## 3. Procedure

Flash the new build, then replicate `scope_measure.c`'s period algorithm on the
host against the same buffer the badge reads, and compare `fs / period_samples`
against the commanded frequency.

The algorithm being replicated (`scope_measure.c:96-137`): hysteresis about the
mid level at ±5% of span, count rising crossings, period = (last rise − first
rise) / (rises − 1).

**A method error, caught mid-experiment and worth recording.** The first pass
read through `spi3 opread`. The badge does not — it reads
`fpga_get_ch1_buf()`, the acquisition task's buffer, filled by a fast /2 read.
At ≥30 kS/s `opread` laps the buffer mid-read, so that pass was measuring a
torn record the badge never sees. Re-run through `spi3 read`, which dumps the
acq buffer. **Testing through a different path than the one under test is this
project's oldest recurring mistake and it very nearly landed again.**

**The estimator finds more rising crossings than the signal contains**, always,
and the error is always positive. The surplus is roughly constant in absolute
terms (2–3 extra crossings), so it is devastating when the record holds few
cycles: at 0x0E/100 Hz the signal has 1.6 cycles and the estimator reports 4.

Widening the hysteresis does not rescue it. At 0x0F/100 Hz, where 3.4 cycles
are expected: ±5% → 15 crossings, ±20% → 12, ±35% → 9.

**And no simple cycle-count gate works either.** Compare 0x10/250 Hz (17.2
cycles, +6.7%) against 0x0F/500 Hz (16.9 cycles, +24.6%) — near-identical
cycle counts, error different by a factor of four. The timebase code matters
beyond how many cycles are in the record, which points at the record's own
quality at higher rates rather than at the arithmetic.

## 6. Blind spots

- **Does not identify the source of the surplus crossings.** Noise chatter at
  the threshold, the rolling engine's wrap seam, and low-frequency wander are
  all consistent with what is seen; none is established.
- **One channel, one range, one amplitude, one session.**
- **Only three codes**, all in the measured tier.
- **Does not test whether a spectral method would do better on the MCU** — that
  is inferred from the fact that the sample rate itself was measured to 0.1% by
  peak search, not demonstrated in firmware.
- The host replication of the estimator is a transcription; it matches the C by
  reading, not by execution.

## 7. Conclusion

- **Established:** `Freq = fs / period_samples` is not fit to print. Measured
  +1.6% to +112% against a known drive.
- **Blame assigned:** the sample rate is not at fault. It is independently
  confirmed five ways and fold-checked; the error is entirely in
  `period_samples`, which over-counts rising crossings.
- **Action taken:** the Freq badge is reverted to blank and Period is back to
  SAMPLES rather than seconds. The conversion to seconds is one multiply and
  the rate is sound — but dressing a 5–50% wrong number in real units makes it
  more convincing, not more correct.
- **What survives, and it is the real win:** the **seconds-per-division label**
  in the status bar and the timebase popup. That is derived purely from the
  measured rate and the renderer's geometry, with no estimator involved, so it
  is sound and it stays. `fpga scope cal` also dumps the whole rate table.
- **NOT excluded:** that the estimator is fine and the *record* is at fault at
  higher rates. The 0x10-vs-0x0F asymmetry at equal cycle counts hints that
  way.
- **Follow-up:** (1) find the surplus crossings — plot a record at 0x0E/100 Hz
  and look at what crosses the threshold; (2) replace edge counting with a
  spectral peak search, which the build already has CMSIS-DSP for and which
  measured the sample rate to 0.1%; (3) re-run this experiment as the
  acceptance test for whichever fix lands.
- **Method note:** the first pass of this experiment read through `opread`
  while the badge reads the acq buffer. Caught before it produced a
  conclusion, but only just — verify through the path under test, not a
  convenient one.
