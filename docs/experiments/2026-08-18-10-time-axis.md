# EXP-10 — the time axis, and the double FFT that hid it

> **⚠ RESCALED 2026-08-19 by [EXP-14](2026-08-19-14-siggen-sample-rate.md).**
> Every sample rate below is **1.21× too high.** The fits used the frequency
> *commanded* from the ESP32 source; the source delivers **0.8250×** that,
> because its DDS loop reschedules from `now` after the work is done and
> free-runs at 32,999 S/s against an assumed 40,000. Measured against the
> ESP32's own crystal, and corroborated by stock's frequency counter and by
> Stlkv's independent rig.
>
> **Nothing else here is affected.** The error is purely multiplicative, so
> every R², the linearity, the 1-2-5 shape of the ladder and both fold

## 1. Problem

EXP-08 §6 recorded that the capture "does not resolve frequency at all": 100,
250, 500 and 1000 Hz all peaked at spectrum **bin 1 with magnitude ~0.1**,
where a coherent 36-count sine should show a line around 3. That claim
propagated into `CLAUDE.md`, the 2026-08-18 devlog, `scope_ui.c` and
`scope_cal.h` as "the time axis is not merely unscaled but untrustworthy", and
it is what blocks Freq, period-in-seconds and any rise-time measurement.

This experiment set out to chase that anomaly.

## 2. Hypothesis

The record is a uniformly-sampled time series. If it is, the spectral peak bin
must be linear in drive frequency through the origin, `bin = f · 1024/fs`, and
`1024/slope` is the sample rate.

- **If true:** a fit over tones inside the resolvable band is linear with small
  residuals, and gives a single consistent `fs`.
- **If false:** no single `fs` explains the set, and the residuals are large —
  the samples are not uniformly spaced and no timebase register will fix it.

## 3. Procedure

`scripts/bench.py` on `/dev/ttyACM0`, ESP32 generator on `/dev/ttyUSB0`.
CH1 only (CH2 parked), frontend range 6, offset centred, 2000 mVpp, settle 1.0
–1.4 s per point (≥ one capture buffer). Peak found by `peaks(v, 1)` — a
search, never a fixed bin.

Two read paths were used:

| path | how | why |
|---|---|---|
| `spi3 opread 0x04` | SSPI at /256, ~35 ms per window | the standing method |
| `spi3 read 1024` | dumps the acquisition task's CH1 buffer, filled by a /2 read | far tighter snapshot; needed once the engine laps the buffer mid-read |

Fits are forced through the origin (bin 0 *is* DC) and computed only over
points whose peak rose above the noise floor — a bin read off noise is not a
measurement of anything.

## 5. Results

### 5a. The EXP-08 anomaly is an artifact of its own analysis code

`ladder2.py:44` read:

```python
pk = peaks(spectrum(v), 1)[0]
```

`peaks()` calls `spectrum()` internally (`bench.py:729`). So the record was
transformed **twice**. Pushing clean synthetic tones through the same call:

| synthetic tone | `peaks(v)` | `peaks(spectrum(v))` |
|---|---|---|
| bin 17 | (17, 20.00) | **(1, 0.04)** |
| bin 34 | (34, 20.00) | **(1, 0.04)** |
| bin 68 | (68, 20.00) | **(1, 0.04)** |

A double transform returns bin 1, magnitude 0.04, for **every** input
frequency. That is EXP-08's reported signature — "all peak at bin 1 with
magnitude ~0.1" — reproduced exactly, with no hardware involved.

### 5b. The time axis is linear

Same build, same standing timebase, single transform:

| f (Hz) | 100 | 150 | 250 | 400 | 600 | 900 | 1300 | 1800 | 2500 | 3500 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bin | 6 | 13 | 19 | 31 | 42 | 60 | 91 | 127 | 168 | 241 |

**fs = 14,890 S/s, R² = 0.9990, max residual 3.9 bins over n = 10.**

Four independent fits of the same code agree within 0.5%:

| fit | points | path | fs | R² |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10-point band | 100–3500 Hz | opread | 14,890 | 0.9990 |
| 5-point | 250–3500 Hz | opread | 14,866 | 0.9992 |
| 5-point (repeat) | 250–3500 Hz | opread | 14,854 | 0.9994 |
| 5-point | 250–3500 Hz | acq buffer | 14,811 | 0.9994 |

### 5c. Why the low-frequency end still looks broken (and is not)

Step 1 swept 0.5–1000 Hz and the bins below ~55 Hz were erratic small numbers.
That is arithmetic, not a fault: at 14.9 kS/s, a 5 Hz tone is bin **0.34** —
below the first bin. Every tone under about 15 Hz is sub-bin-1 by construction,
so the "peak" found is drift. The resolvable band at this code starts around
30 Hz.

### 5d. The reg 0x01 ladder, and what limits it

`opread` fits collapse above ~30 kS/s. That is the read, not the device: the
SSPI window clocks at /256 and takes ~35 ms, and prior bench work (2026-08-15)
already recorded that it is **lapped at >=30 kS/s** — the engine overwrites the
buffer while the read is still running. The R2 collapse begins exactly at that
documented threshold. Re-reading the same codes through the acquisition task's
buffer (a /2 read, far tighter snapshot) recovers them:

| reg `0x01` | fs via `opread` | R2 | fs via acq buffer | R2 | tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `0x08` | 1,660 | 0.862 | — | — | **suspect (see below)** |
| `0x10` | 14,854-14,890 | 0.999 | 14,811 | 0.9994 | **measured** |
| `0x0F` | 29,837 | 0.984 | **30,235** | 0.9895 | **measured** |
| `0x0E` | 54,415 | 0.832 | **62,958** | 0.9804 | **measured** |
| `0x0D` | 122,340 | -1.33 | **150,706** | 0.9040 | provisional |
| `0x0C` | 99,359 | -1.96 | 296,215 | 0.602 | unmeasured |
| `0x0B` | 35,753 | -1.43 | 618,496 | -0.054 | unmeasured |
| `0x0A` | 8,418 | -4.37 | 757,961 | -1.03 | unmeasured |

The acq-path numbers for `0x0F`/`0x0E`/`0x0D` land on **30 k / 63 k / 151 k**,
independently reproducing the 2026-08-15 estimates of "~30 k / ~60 k / ~150 k"
— which had been recorded as indicative, without fits. They now have an R2.

**What limits `0x0C` and faster is the SOURCE, not the device.** The ESP32 tops
out near 4.5 kHz usable, which at 300 kS/s is bin 15 and at 600 kS/s is bin 7.
Fitting a line through bins of 1-15 measures quantisation. The estimates happen
to sit near the earlier 300 k / 500-600 k / 1 M figures, but an R2 of -0.05
means the fit explains nothing and the number must not be used. These need a
higher-frequency source — precisely what the incoming USB signal generator is
for.

**`0x08` — WITHDRAWN 2026-08-18 by [EXP-12](2026-08-18-12-code-08-incoherent.md).**
The paragraph below stands as written but its number is void: a dense 14-tone
resweep showed the record at `0x08` does not reproduce between reads (one
unchanged tone gave peak bins [171, 132, 104]), two passes of the same sweep
disagreed by 7.6%, and the fitted rate missed its own above-Nyquist fold
predictions by up to 227 bins where the same test at `0x10` missed by 8. There
is no sample rate to measure at this code, so 1,660 S/s — like the earlier
1.07 kS/s — is an artifact. Original text kept:

> **`0x08` is unresolved and its number here should not be adopted.** Refitted
below its own Nyquist (40-420 Hz) it gives 1,660 S/s at R2 0.862, and five of
the six points are consistent with ~1,780 S/s while the 200 Hz point implies
1,067. An earlier session recorded this code at **1.07 kS/s**. Dropping the
outlier to make the fit agree would be exactly the move this project has been
burned by, so it stays in and the code stays flagged. It is also the power-on
default, which makes resolving it worth a dedicated pass.



## 6. Blind spots

- **This does not validate the absolute rate**, only that it is constant and
  that bin is linear in frequency. `fs` is derived from the ESP32's *commanded*
  frequency, whose readback is known to be ~0.82× off — so a scale error in the
  source becomes a scale error in `fs`, exactly as it does for volts. The
  linearity and the R² are unaffected by that; the number 14,890 is not.
- **One channel, one range, one build.** CH2's rate was not measured, and
  nothing here shows the two channels share a clock.
- **The fits do not distinguish a genuinely non-uniform sampler from a uniform
  one**, if the non-uniformity is periodic with the buffer. A pathological
  pattern could still produce a linear bin-vs-f relation.
- **The reg-0x01 codes above ~30 kS/s are limited by the READ, not measured to
  be broken.** A negative R² there is a statement about the instrument, and
  saying otherwise would repeat this experiment's own lesson.
- **Nothing here tests the trigger.** All of this is free-run.

## 7. Conclusion

- **REFUTED — EXP-08 §6's "the capture does not resolve frequency".** It was a
  double FFT in the measuring script. The finding is withdrawn in place there,
  and corrected in `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/devlog/2026-08-18-*`, `scope_ui.c` and
  `scope_cal.h`, all of which had inherited it.
- **Also withdrawn: the corroboration.** "Span is identical across timebases"
  was a true measurement but was never evidence for the claim — span is an
  amplitude statistic and is *expected* to be rate-invariant. A non-observation
  was read as confirmation, which is how a single analysis bug became a
  hardware finding in four files.
- **The lag-1 autocorrelation figures were right all along, and pointed the
  other way.** At 14.9 kS/s a 250 Hz tone gives 59.6 samples per cycle, so
  lag-1 should be cos(2π/59.6) = 0.994 — against 0.99 measured. That number was
  quantitatively consistent with a *working* time axis at the moment it was
  filed as evidence of a broken one.
- **Established:** the capture record is a uniformly-sampled time series, and
  the sample rate is measurable to better than 1% by fitting bin against
  frequency.
- **Established:** the timebase is SPI reg `0x01`, and it moves the rate.
- **NOT excluded (explicitly):** absolute rate accuracy, CH2's rate, trigger
  behaviour, and whether the fast codes are usable once read properly.
- **Follow-up:** (1) finish the per-code rate table with a read path that keeps
  up, and tier it the way `scope_cal.c` tiers volts; (2) plumb the live reg-0x01
  code through to `scope_ui.c` so Freq and period-in-seconds can be printed
  from a measured rate rather than an assumed one; (3) re-measure `0x08`, whose
  fit here is the weakest and disagrees with an earlier estimate (see 5d).
- **Guard added:** `bench.py` now refuses a magnitude spectrum handed back in as
  samples — odd length, non-negative, DC exactly zero — with a message naming
  the mistake. Two selftest checks cover it (50 checks, 0 failures).
